Can I handle Washington probate if I live out of state?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

A person living outside Washington may be able to serve as personal representative of a Washington estate by designating a qualified Washington resident agent and satisfying the other eligibility requirements. Living elsewhere does not automatically prevent appointment.

Resident agent

Under RCW 11.36.010, the resident agent generally resides in the county of administration, with an exception for the attorney of record who may reside elsewhere in Washington. The designation is filed with the opening paperwork and signed by the relevant parties.

What can be done from another state?

Gather the will, death certificate, asset information and contact details for heirs. Discuss filing, appearance requirements, oath and document signatures with a Washington attorney before arranging travel. Court procedures vary; do not assume every step can be remote.

If a home is involved

Identify the deed, occupants, insurance and loans. The representative's authority to sell or transfer the home may depend on title and the court order.

Eligibility remains important

Other statutory disqualifications and appointment priorities still apply, regardless of residence.

Common questions

Do I have to fly to Washington?

Not necessarily, but the court, documents and particular estate determine whether an appearance is needed. Confirm before making travel plans.

Can my local lawyer handle Washington probate?

Ask whether they are authorized to handle the Washington proceeding or whether Washington counsel is needed.

Can a lawyer be my resident agent?

RCW 11.36.010 provides an attorney-of-record exception to the usual county residence requirement. Discuss the arrangement with counsel.

Washington legal sources

Relevant provisions of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW):

  • RCW 11.28.120
