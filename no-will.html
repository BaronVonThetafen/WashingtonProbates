What happens when someone dies without a will in Washington?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

If a Washington resident dies without a will, the probate estate is distributed under Washington's intestate succession statutes. The court can appoint an administrator to collect assets, resolve debts and distribute property; the state does not automatically inherit everything.

Who inherits?

The statutory shares depend on surviving family members and whether property is community or separate property. Identify the entire family tree before calculating shares.

Who can administer?

The statute provides appointment priorities. Higher-priority relatives may be able to waive priority. The archive's June 2026 update identifies a change from 40 to 90 days for certain appointment priorities, not a deadline to open probate.

What must the petition explain?

The updated source describes requirements to explain the basis for believing no will exists, efforts to identify heirs, eligibility to serve and estate assets. A bond may be required.

Nonintervention powers

Waivers or a noticed hearing may be needed to obtain these powers. Appointment alone does not necessarily confer them.

Common questions

Can an adult child apply to serve?

Potentially, subject to priority, eligibility and waivers; a surviving spouse may have priority.

Do we need to identify heirs even if everyone gets along?

Yes. The correct heirs must be identified for notice, appointment and distribution.
