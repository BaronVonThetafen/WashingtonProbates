Which assets pass outside probate in Washington?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

Some property transfers at death through a beneficiary designation, trust, survivorship arrangement or other legal instrument rather than the will. Review the actual account or title documents before deciding whether court appointment is necessary.

Bank and investment accounts

A payable-on-death or transfer-on-death designation may direct the holder to pay the named beneficiary. Review the precise account agreement.

Retirement and insurance

A valid beneficiary designation can allow direct claims. If the estate is the beneficiary or no effective designation exists, the provider's default terms matter. Retirement tax rules require specialized advice.

Real estate and community property

Survivorship title, a transfer-on-death deed or a community property agreement may provide a route outside probate; verify recording and title requirements.

Trust property

A successor trustee may manage property actually titled to the trust. Property not transferred into the trust may still require probate. Trust administration still involves fiduciary duties.

Common questions

Does a beneficiary designation always override the will?

The asset's controlling instrument and applicable law matter; avoid assuming all designations work the same way.

Are nonprobate assets always exempt from claims or taxes?

No. Creditor treatment and tax inclusion vary by asset and circumstances.
