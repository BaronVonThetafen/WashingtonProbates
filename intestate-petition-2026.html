What changed in Washington probate law in June 2026?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

The June 6, 2026 King County Probates article describes amendments effective June 11, 2026, including expanded intestate petition disclosures, eligibility restrictions and a longer appointment priority period. The changes should be checked against the enacted statutes before filing.

Document the investigation

For a petition without a will, explain the basis for believing there is no will, the effort to find heirs, eligibility and the investigation into estate assets. Disclosure requirements may depend on the applicant's relationship to the deceased and the need to set bond.

Appointment eligibility

The archived update describes restrictions involving recent revocation of letters, findings of dishonesty or fiduciary breach, and certain conflicts involving major estate assets.

Priority period

The article describes an extension from 40 to 90 days for certain priority applicants. This is not a general deadline for starting probate.

Reporting after appointment

The author distinguishes new reporting provisions in chapter 11.76 RCW from nonintervention administration under chapter 11.68. Confirm applicability to the specific order and estate.

Common questions

Can I use an older petition form?

Have counsel check that it contains the currently required facts and verification.

Is every estate subject to the new reporting provisions?

The source author interprets some reporting provisions as directed to full-intervention estates. This is an interpretation to verify, not a universal rule.
