What changed in Washington probate law in June 2026?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

Washington probate amendments effective June 11, 2026 address intestate petition disclosures, eligibility restrictions and appointment priority periods. Check the applicable enacted statutes before filing.

Document the investigation

For a petition without a will, explain the basis for believing there is no will, the effort to find heirs, eligibility and the investigation into estate assets. Disclosure requirements may depend on the applicant's relationship to the deceased and the need to set bond.

Appointment eligibility

Appointment eligibility restrictions may involve recent revocation of letters, findings of dishonesty or fiduciary breach, and certain conflicts involving major estate assets.

Priority period

The 2026 amendments extend the priority period from 40 to 90 days for certain applicants. This is not a general deadline for starting probate.

Reporting after appointment

Reporting provisions in chapter 11.76 RCW should be read alongside the nonintervention administration provisions in chapter 11.68. Confirm applicability to the specific order and estate.

Common questions

Can I use an older petition form?

Have counsel check that it contains the currently required facts and verification.

Is every estate subject to the new reporting provisions?

Some reporting provisions may apply differently to full-intervention and nonintervention estates. Confirm which provisions govern the particular estate.
