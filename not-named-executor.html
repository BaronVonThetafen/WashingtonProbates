Can I administer an estate if I am not named executor in the will?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

Someone not nominated in a will may be appointed as administrator with the will annexed if the nominated individuals cannot or do not serve and statutory appointment requirements are met.

In this guide

  • Understand priority timing
  • Ask for powers and address bond
  • Administer according to the will
  • Common questions

Check the nomination

Review alternate nominees, any renunciation and whether people with priority intend to serve. Do not treat nomination alone as appointment.

Understand priority timing

The source contains an older 40-day reference and an updated 90-day reference; its 2026 update describes the extended period. Verify current law before applying.

Ask for powers and address bond

The process may require waivers or advance notice and a bond. Confirm whether nonintervention powers are available.

Administer according to the will

An administrator with the will annexed generally still follows the admitted will's directions for distribution, while assuming the ordinary fiduciary duties.

Common questions

Does the estate become intestate just because the executor declines?

No. If the will is admitted, its distribution provisions can remain controlling.

What documents prove my appointment?

The court may direct issuance of letters of administration with will annexed.
