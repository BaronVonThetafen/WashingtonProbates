What if a Washington probate beneficiary is a minor?

Prepared by Ruth Apahidean West, Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

A minor or an adult unable to protect their own interests may need a court-appointed guardian ad litem in a probate. The appointment and scope depend on the circumstances and applicable rules.

Identify affected people

Tell the court and counsel about minors and adults with impaired capacity, including any existing legal guardian. They may be heirs even when not named in the will.

Role of a probate GAL

A guardian ad litem protects the person's interests and may review the administration and proposed distribution, reporting to the court as required.

Limited appointments

The scope of a probate GAL appointment may be full or limited, depending on the court order. Confirm current local forms and whether a narrower role is appropriate.

Protect the distribution

Determine whether the will creates a trust or another lawful arrangement for a minor's inheritance. Do not simply pay funds to a parent without authority.

Common questions

Can a parent automatically act as the probate GAL?

Do not assume a parent is eligible to act as probate GAL. The court may appoint an independent person.

What if the inheritance goes to a trust?

A trust can change how property is distributed, but may not remove the need to protect the minor's interests in the probate.
