Who needs notice after a Washington probate is opened?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

Washington requires notice to interested persons and, in applicable cases, government agencies. Appointment notice, creditor-related notice, inventory requests and closing notices are separate obligations.

Within 20 days of appointment

RCW 11.28.237 addresses notice to heirs, will beneficiaries and nonprobate beneficiaries. This can include heirs who are disinherited. Confirm the statutory recipients and method for the particular estate.

Agency notices

Separate agency notice duties may apply, including a DSHS notice within 30 days when the notice-to-creditors process is not used and a Department of Revenue notice within 60 days if the decedent was engaged in business. Check the agency's current procedures.

Inventory and closing notices

An inventory must be prepared within the applicable period and furnished when legally required. Separate notice rules apply when filing a declaration of completion.

Proof of compliance

Keep a dated record of service and mailing and file declarations when required. Avoid including sensitive identifiers in public court documents.

Common questions

Does someone disinherited in the will receive notice?

Statutory heirs generally still receive appointment notice even if excluded by the will.

Does waiving a hearing waive all later notices?

No. A waiver applies to its scope; check later notice obligations separately.

Washington legal sources

Relevant provisions of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW):

  • RCW 11.28.237
  • RCW 11.44.015
  • RCW 82.32.240
  • RCW 11.68.110
