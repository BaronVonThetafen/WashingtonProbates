How do I prepare an inventory of a Washington estate?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

The estate inventory identifies property and values it as of the date of death. It is distinct from an accounting of later payments, sales and distributions. The archived guide describes a three-month preparation deadline after appointment.

In this guide

  • Use date-of-death values

Identify assets

Gather deeds, bank and investment records, vehicle titles, insurance information and business-interest records. Distinguish estate property from property passing directly to a beneficiary.

Use date-of-death values

Request statements for financial accounts and appropriate valuations for real property or valuable personal property. Keep the supporting records.

Keep estate funds separate

An estate bank account can help track receipts and expenses. Institutions may ask for letters, a death certificate and the estate's tax identification number.

Respond to requests

The archive explains that the inventory need not routinely be filed in court but must be provided to qualifying requesters within the statutory time. Verify who is entitled to receive it.

Common questions

Should I include funeral bills in the inventory?

They are expenses, not date-of-death asset values. Keep them in the estate accounting.

Can I deposit estate funds in my personal account?

Keep estate money separate and maintain clear records.
