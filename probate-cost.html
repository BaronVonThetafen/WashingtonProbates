How the initial probate meeting works

Start by contacting Queen Anne Law, PLLC. Ruth first determines whether your situation appears to be one in which she can assist. If probate services appear appropriate, she arranges an initial fact-finding meeting to learn about the estate, discuss your needs and assess the likely scope of work. The meeting is billed only if you decide to move forward with her. If you choose not to hire the firm, you are not billed for that meeting.

Hourly probate services

An hourly arrangement may be a good fit when the amount of legal work is uncertain, an estate is complex, or unexpected issues could arise. Ruth will discuss the applicable rates and the proposed scope of work before you decide whether to retain the firm.

Flat-fee probate services

For estates with a reasonably predictable scope of work, Ruth may propose a flat fee for specified probate services. You can discuss what is included, what is excluded and how changes in scope would be handled before deciding whether to hire the firm.

Hybrid probate fees — a flat fee with flexibility

A hybrid arrangement may include a flat fee covering a defined scope of work or up to an agreed number of attorney hours. If complications arise and the probate requires additional time beyond those hours, extra work is billed under the agreed terms. This approach can provide a measure of budget predictability without assuming every estate will require the same amount of legal work.

What affects the total cost of probate?

The amount of legal work can depend on the estate’s assets, real estate, the availability of a will, creditor issues, disagreements among beneficiaries, tax matters and the representative’s needs. At the fact-finding meeting, Ruth can discuss which billing arrangement is appropriate for your circumstances.

Other estate expenses

Court filing, letters, publication, bond, appraisals and tax preparation may be separate expenses. Actual amounts depend on the case and current provider charges. Ask which expenses are included in any proposed fee.

Who pays the probate attorney?

Reasonable legal expenses incurred in estate administration may be paid or reimbursed by the estate, subject to applicable law. A representative must use estate funds responsibly; confirm payment terms in writing.

Common questions

Can probate work be billed hourly?

Yes. Depending on the estate and anticipated work, the firm may propose an hourly arrangement, a flat fee, or a hybrid. Ruth can discuss specific rates and terms in the initial meeting.

Do I have to pay for the initial probate meeting if I do not hire Ruth?

No. After an initial screening, Ruth offers an initial fact-finding meeting for prospective probate clients whose matters appear to be a fit. The meeting is billed only if you decide to move forward with her.

Does Queen Anne Law offer flat-fee probate?

Yes, a flat-fee arrangement may be offered for a suitable estate and defined scope of services. Ruth can discuss whether that approach makes sense at the fact-finding meeting.

What is a hybrid probate fee?

An agreed flat fee covers up to a specified number of attorney hours. If complications require additional time beyond that amount, extra work is billed as provided in the engagement agreement.

Can I discuss fees before deciding whether to hire Ruth?

Yes. The initial fact-finding meeting is an opportunity to discuss the estate, anticipated scope of legal work and whether hourly, flat-fee or hybrid billing is suitable before deciding to proceed.

Does every probate cost the same amount?

No. The overall cost depends on the agreed billing arrangement, the work required, the complexity of the estate, and expenses outside attorney fees. Ruth can discuss anticipated fees after learning more about the matter.

Are probate and estate planning fees handled the same way?

Probate and estate planning matters can involve different services and fee arrangements. Under the firm’s current initial-consultation policy, the meeting is billed only if you decide to retain the firm. Ruth can explain the proposed terms before you proceed.
