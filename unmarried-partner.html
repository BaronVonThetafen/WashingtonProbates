What rights does an unmarried partner have when someone dies in Washington?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

An unmarried surviving partner may not have the same statutory inheritance protections as a spouse. Washington recognizes committed intimate relationships in certain property disputes, but a potential equitable property claim is different from an automatic right to inherit.

Identify the relationship and legal status

A committed intimate relationship and a state-registered domestic partnership are distinct. Review the actual legal status, will, title and agreements.

Separate ownership from inheritance

A surviving partner may assert an interest in property acquired during the relationship independent of any inheritance. Washington case law, including Connell v. Francisco, addresses property claims arising from committed intimate relationships.

Protect evidence and housing

Gather title, account, contribution and relationship records. Do not assume the estate can exclude a partner or that the partner owns all shared property.

Seek timely advice

Potential claims may involve probate, property law and deadlines. A will, beneficiary designations and estate planning can also help partners address future uncertainty.

Common questions

Does living together make me an heir?

Not automatically. Intestacy rights and equitable property claims are different.

Is a registered domestic partner treated the same as an unmarried cohabitant?

No. Registered partnerships have statutory protections that ordinary cohabitation may not provide.

Washington legal sources

Relevant provisions of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW):

  • RCW 11.04.015
  • RCW 26.60.100
  • RCW 11.40.110
