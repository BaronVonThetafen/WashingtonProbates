What if I only have a copy of a Washington will?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

A copy of a will may sometimes be admitted, but a missing original can raise a presumption of revocation. Establishing the terms, execution and circumstances of the missing document can require evidence and a court hearing.

In this guide
  • Search for the original
  • Preserve evidence
  • Compare inheritance outcomes
  • Prepare for a hearing

Search for the original

Contact the drafting attorney, check personal files and identify any safe deposit box. Ask counsel about lawful access rather than trying to enter a box without authority.

Preserve evidence

Keep the copy, records of the search, communications with the decedent and witness contact information. These may be relevant to whether the testator intended revocation.

Compare inheritance outcomes

The archived article suggests comparing the copy's distributions with intestacy law. Do not choose to disregard a will without legal review; beneficiaries, powers and other provisions may differ.

Prepare for a hearing

The source describes a heightened evidentiary standard and witness affidavits or declarations. An attorney can assess whether a lost-will petition is appropriate.

Common questions

Does a copy automatically count as the will?

No. Court admission and sufficient proof may be required.

What if the original is in a bank box?

Ask counsel about the current local process for authorized access to look for a will.
