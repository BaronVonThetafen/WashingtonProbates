Serving King County and Pierce County

Ruth is based in Seattle and also spends time in Pierce County. She is available to discuss probate matters involving Seattle, Bellevue, Tacoma, Puyallup, Gig Harbor and other communities in King and Pierce counties. Whether you live nearby or outside Washington, contact her to discuss the estate and whether she can assist.

Probate assistance throughout Washington State

You do not need to live in Seattle or have an estate in King or Pierce County to inquire. Ruth can assist with Washington probate matters across the state and can associate with other attorneys when local counsel or an appearance in another county is needed. The approach depends on the court, the estate, any required appearances and the agreed scope of representation.

When a consultation is particularly helpful

Consider legal advice when title to a home is unclear, there is no will, a bank requires letters, the representative lives out of state, beneficiaries disagree or the estate may be insolvent.

What to ask an attorney

Ask about the necessary filings, court authority, notice deadlines, costs, and how legal work will be divided between counsel and the representative. Discuss any local court requirements, potential coordination with other counsel and the scope of representation.

About Queen Anne Law

Ruth Apahidean West holds undergraduate degrees in English and History and a Juris Doctor from the University of Washington. The practice also assists with wills, trusts and powers of attorney. For estate planning, Ruth’s usual hourly rate is $450, and she offers a discounted initial consultation. Estate planning consultation terms are separate from her probate fact-finding meeting policy.

Flexible probate fee arrangements

Depending on the matter, Queen Anne Law, PLLC may offer hourly billing, a flat fee for defined services, or a hybrid fee covering an agreed number of hours with additional work handled under the engagement agreement. Ruth’s usual hourly probate rate is $475. Discuss the appropriate arrangement during the initial fact-finding meeting, which is billed only if you decide to move forward with her.

How the first probate meeting works

Ruth first assesses whether your matter appears to fit her services. If so, she arranges a fact-finding meeting to discuss the estate, legal needs and possible fee arrangements. If you decide not to move forward with her, you are not billed for that meeting.

Prepare for the first appointment

Bring available estate documents, a list of assets and debts, names of potential heirs, and your main questions. Do not send confidential details through an unsecured channel.
