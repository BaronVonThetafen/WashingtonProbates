Washington probate · Managing an estate

What are the duties of a Washington personal representative?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

A personal representative must safeguard estate property, identify heirs and beneficiaries, provide notices, manage claims and taxes, keep records, distribute property properly and close the estate. Court appointment and nonintervention powers do not remove fiduciary duties.

Before appointment

Locate the original will, death certificate, family information and ownership records. Determine whether probate is necessary and what powers to request.

After appointment

Obtain letters, calendar notices, secure assets, locate creditors, arrange date-of-death values and establish a separate estate account as appropriate.

During administration

Keep a ledger and receipts, review claim validity and priority, coordinate tax work and communicate with interested parties. Do not mix estate money with personal funds.

Before distribution

Resolve debts and taxes or reserve appropriately. Prepare an inventory, account for distributions and follow the closing process for the estate's powers.

Common questions

Is the inventory the same as an accounting?

No. The inventory values estate assets at death; an accounting describes later receipts, expenses and distributions.

Do I have to keep records even if the heirs agree?

Yes. Agreement does not remove the representative's responsibilities.
