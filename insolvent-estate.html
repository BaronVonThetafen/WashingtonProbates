What if a Washington estate owes more than it owns?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

An insolvent estate has insufficient assets to pay its obligations. Relatives do not ordinarily become liable for a decedent's debts merely by inheriting, but handling estate assets or paying creditors incorrectly can create risks.

Do not distribute assets prematurely

Identify assets, secured debts, unsecured claims and expenses before removing or transferring property. Check any separate personal obligations.

Consider whether to administer

Possible options include declining to administer, negotiating claims and opening a court-supervised probate. Each has consequences; waiting for claims to expire is not a universally safe strategy.

Creditor priority and releases

Get advice on the statutory order of payments and any proposed settlement. Avoid personally promising payment or releasing valuable property without authority.

Court supervision

An insolvent estate may not qualify for nonintervention powers. Greater court involvement can make even a small estate complex.

Common questions

Do I have to pay my parent's credit cards?

Not simply because you are their child. A joint obligation or guarantee may change the answer.

Can I use a small estate affidavit?

The affidavit requires debts to be paid or provided for, so insolvency can prevent its use.

Washington legal sources

  • RCW 11.40.051
