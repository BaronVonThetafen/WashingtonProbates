How long does probate take in Washington?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

There is no single duration for every Washington probate. Timing depends on obtaining authority, collecting property, resolving creditor claims, preparing taxes, distributing assets and completing the closing procedure.

Appointment and early notices

The initial petition and documents establish authority. After appointment, notices and the inventory have their own deadlines; track them from the correct triggering event.

Creditor and will contest periods

Publication of a notice to creditors can start a four-month claims period, with additional rules for actual notice. Admission of a will also starts a statutory contest period. These periods are separate and should not be confused.

Asset and tax work

A house sale, beneficiary disagreement, difficult-to-value assets or outstanding tax returns may extend the time needed.

Closing

A nonintervention estate may often close by declaration. Court-supervised estates use different procedures and may need a hearing.

Common questions

Is four months the minimum for all probate cases?

No. Four-month periods arise in specific contexts; the actual closing date depends on the estate and legal requirements.

What can delay closure?

Unresolved debts, title problems, contested inheritances, taxes and sale of assets are common reasons.
