Can a will that is not self-proving be admitted in Washington?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

A will without an adequate self-proving witness affidavit is not necessarily invalid. It may need additional proof of execution before the court will admit it to probate.

Review the document

Check signatures, witness information and any attestation provision. Different rules may apply to electronic wills; confirm the type and execution requirements.

Locate witnesses

The archived guide describes obtaining sworn statements from witnesses about execution and apparent competency. Preserve contact details and avoid coaching witnesses.

If witnesses cannot be found

The source discusses RCW 11.20.040 as a possible route involving satisfactory proof of signatures or other evidence. The court decides whether proof is sufficient.

Consider the consequences

Whether the will can be admitted affects who inherits, who may administer and other terms. Seek legal advice before proceeding as if there were no will.

Common questions

Is notarization alone enough?

Do not assume so; will execution and proof requirements are distinct.

Does a non-self-proving will need a lawyer?

Legal help is particularly useful if witnesses are missing or validity is contested.
