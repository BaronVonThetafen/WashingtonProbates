What are nonintervention powers in Washington probate?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

Nonintervention powers allow a court-appointed representative to manage many estate affairs without seeking a separate court order for each routine transaction. They do not eliminate duties to heirs, beneficiaries or creditors.

  • Limits

Why request them?

They can simplify estate administration, including collecting or selling property and paying obligations, subject to law and the appointment order.

How to obtain them

A qualifying will nomination may make the request simpler. When there is no will or the applicant was not nominated, waivers or a noticed hearing may be necessary.

Limits

An insolvent estate, a restrictive will, disputes or other circumstances may affect whether powers are granted or retained. A court may still intervene under applicable law.

Closing implications

A representative with nonintervention powers may be able to use a declaration of completion; a full-intervention estate requires different procedures.

Common questions

Do letters automatically grant nonintervention powers?

No. Review the court order and statutory requirements.

Can a representative ignore notices if the court is not supervising?

No. Notice, inventory, creditor and fiduciary obligations remain important.
