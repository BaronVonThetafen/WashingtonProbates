How do you close a Washington probate estate?

Prepared for Queen Anne Law · Attorney review pending

Quick answer

Closing generally follows settlement of debts and taxes, preparation of records and distribution of estate property. A nonintervention estate often uses a declaration of completion; other estates may require a court hearing and distribution order.

Check readiness

Confirm the estate has addressed claims, taxes, fees and property transfers. Retain adequate records and determine whether a reserve is still needed.

Declaration of completion

The source describes filing a declaration with the required information, including fees and distributions. If notice has not been properly waived, send the required documents to interested parties within the applicable time.

Receipts and objections

Properly drafted receipts and waivers can simplify closure, but the source also describes a 30-day objection period when notice is required. Review the statutory process before relying on a waiver.

Court-supervised alternatives

A decree of distribution or final report and petition may be appropriate depending on the representative's authority. Do not use a nonintervention procedure in an estate lacking those powers.

Common questions

Can I close without another hearing?

Often, with nonintervention powers and a properly completed declaration process.

Does distributing all money automatically close the probate?

No. Follow the closing procedure and retain records showing compliance.
