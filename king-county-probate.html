How do I start probate in King County, Washington?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

King County probate is administered through the superior court. An opening petition, supporting records, proposed order and required oath or bond are among the documents that may be needed. Local filing and hearing procedures should be checked before submitting paperwork.

Prepare the opening documents

Determine whether there is an original will, who seeks appointment and what powers are requested. Assemble the will, death certificate, petition, proposed order and oath as applicable.

Determine whether advance notice is required

A routine self-proving will naming the applicant may have a simpler procedure than an estate without a will or an estate with minors. Waivers can affect hearing requirements.

Check local procedures

Confirm the current superior court ex parte process, filing system, fees, calendar and local rules before filing; older instructions may no longer apply.

After the order

Obtain letters and calendar appointment notices, claims and inventory duties. Retain the case number for filings and record requests.

Common questions

Can I file in King County if I live elsewhere?

Your residence does not necessarily determine venue. Review Washington venue rules and the estate's circumstances with counsel.

Where can I find a case?

The King County Superior Court Clerk provides case records and access procedures; use the official court website.
