How do I sell or transfer an inherited house in Washington?

Prepared by Ruth Apahidean West, Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

An inherited Washington house often requires probate if no other arrangement establishes who can transfer title. A will naming an executor is not the same as a court appointment authorizing that person to act.

In this guide
  • Find the deed and title arrangement
  • Establish authority before selling
  • Establish value and expenses
  • Coordinate with heirs
  • Common questions
  • Legal sources

Find the deed and title arrangement

Check whether the owner held the house individually, jointly with survivorship, in a trust or under a transfer-on-death deed or community property agreement. Do not assume two names on a deed establish survivorship.

Establish authority before selling

If probate is necessary, the appointed representative may be able to sign sale documents under the powers granted in the court order. A title company may require proof of appointment or additional documentation.

Establish value and expenses

Obtain a date-of-death valuation, review mortgages and other liens, and keep a record of costs. Discuss tax basis and sale proceeds with a qualified tax adviser.

Coordinate with heirs

Determine whether a sale or distribution in kind is appropriate under the will, statutory rights and estate obligations. Address occupancy and disagreements early.

Common questions

Can I use a small estate affidavit to transfer a house?

No; the procedure is for personal property, although probate real property is counted in the estate value test.

Do I need a probate if I inherit with siblings?

It depends on title and the transfer mechanism, not merely the number of heirs.

Washington legal sources

  • RCW 11.62.010
