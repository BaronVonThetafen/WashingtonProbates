Can I use a Washington small estate affidavit instead of probate?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

Washington provides an affidavit procedure for qualifying personal property estates. RCW 11.62.010 sets out eligibility requirements, including a $100,000 net probate-estate limit, a 40-day waiting period after death and notice to other successors. A house cannot be transferred using this affidavit.

Check the requirements

The decedent must have been a Washington resident. The total probate estate, wherever located, must be within the statutory limit after applicable liens and exclusions; no appointment petition may be pending or granted, and debts and funeral expenses must be paid or provided for.

Identify successors

Determine who is entitled to the property under the will or intestacy law. Notify other successors in writing and obtain written authorization if collecting their interests.

Observe both waiting periods

Allow at least 40 days after death and at least 10 days after service or mailing of notice to other successors, subject to the statutory requirements. The periods may overlap.

Collect and account

Prepare the affidavit and proof of death for the property holder, send the required copy to DSHS, and retain receipts and distribution records. Follow secure handling procedures for identifying information.

Common questions

Does real estate count toward the limit?

Yes, probate real property counts even though the affidavit does not transfer its title.

Should I use the affidavit if there are unpaid debts?

Not before checking the debt requirement. An insolvent estate raises additional issues.

Washington legal sources

Relevant provisions of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW):

  • RCW 11.62.010
  • RCW 11.20.010
