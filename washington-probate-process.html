How does probate work in Washington State?

Prepared by Queen Anne Law, PLLC

Quick answer

Washington probate is a superior court process in which a personal representative or administrator receives authority to settle an estate: collect assets, address debts and taxes, distribute property and close the case.

Opening the estate

The petition requests an appointment and, if applicable, admission of the will. The court order and required filings allow the clerk to issue letters evidencing the representative's authority.

Managing the estate

The representative identifies interested parties, provides required notices, gathers property, establishes date-of-death values and handles creditors and taxes. Nonintervention powers can reduce the need to return to court for routine decisions.

Distributing and closing

After obligations are addressed, the representative distributes property under the will or intestacy law and uses the appropriate closing procedure. Estates without nonintervention powers generally require more court involvement.

Why the opening order matters

Review whether the order grants nonintervention powers, requires bond and addresses admission of the will. Those terms affect the rest of the administration.

Common questions

Is an executor the same as a personal representative?

Executor is a familiar term; Washington probate commonly uses personal representative or administrator.

Can probate proceed without a will?

Yes. The court appoints an administrator and the statutory inheritance rules determine distribution.

Washington legal sources

Relevant provisions of the Revised Code of Washington (RCW):

  • RCW 11.28.090
  • RCW 11.48.010
  • RCW 11.68.110
